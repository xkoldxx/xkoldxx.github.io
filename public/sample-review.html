IT Review

Harbor Lane Insurance Agency

9 staff · one office · Microsoft 365

Eight things I found, most urgent first. Each one says what I saw, why it matters, and what fixes it. You can fix these yourself, hand them to whoever does your IT, or ask me.

Effort tags: Quick fix = under an hour or two · Small project = a few hours to a day · Ongoing = a habit, not a one-time job

Fix this week

  1. 1. The office router can be logged into from the internet, with the default password

    Quick fix
    Found:
    The router's admin page answers from outside the office, and the password is still the one printed on the sticker.
    Why it matters:
    Anyone who finds it can take over the office network, and automated scanners look for exactly this.
    Fix:
    Turn off remote admin, set a long unique password stored in a password manager, and update the router's firmware.

  2. 2. Everyone uses the office manager's admin password

    Quick fix
    Found:
    The office manager's Microsoft 365 account is a full admin, and its password is shared with three other staff.
    Why it matters:
    One phished login hands over every mailbox and file in the business, and there's no way to tell who did what.
    Fix:
    Give each person their own login, make a separate admin account used only for admin work, and change the shared password.

  3. 3. No 2-step login on email

    Quick fix
    Found:
    None of the 9 email accounts require a code or app approval to sign in.
    Why it matters:
    A stolen password is all it takes to read client email and send fake invoices from your address.
    Fix:
    Turn on 2-step login for every account with an authenticator app, and walk staff through setup in one short session.

Fix this month

  1. 4. Customer details are going into free ChatGPT accounts

    Small project
    Found:
    Several staff paste client names and policy details into personal, free ChatGPT accounts to draft emails.
    Why it matters:
    Free personal accounts may keep what's pasted in, and the business has no control over that data.
    Fix:
    Move to a business AI plan with data controls (for example, Copilot Chat signed in with your Microsoft 365 work account), and give staff a one-page "what's OK to paste" policy.

  2. 5. Backups run, but nobody has ever restored one

    Ongoing
    Found:
    A nightly backup job reports success, but no file or mailbox has ever been restored from it.
    Why it matters:
    A backup you haven't tested is a guess, and you find out it failed on the worst day.
    Fix:
    Do a test restore now, write down the steps, and repeat it every quarter.

  3. 6. One laptop still runs Windows 10

    Small project
    Found:
    The front desk laptop is on Windows 10, which stopped getting security updates in October 2025.
    Why it matters:
    New security holes on that laptop will never be patched.
    Fix:
    Upgrade it to Windows 11 if the hardware supports it, or replace it.

When convenient

  1. 7. Paying for 3 licenses for people who left

    Quick fix
    Found:
    Three Microsoft 365 licenses are still assigned to former staff.
    Why it matters:
    It's money every month for nothing, and old accounts are an easy way in.
    Fix:
    Save anything needed from those mailboxes, block the accounts, and remove the licenses.

  2. 8. The website is hard to use on a phone, and the Google listing is out of date

    Small project
    Found:
    The site needs zooming and sideways scrolling on a phone, and the Google listing shows old hours and a former phone number.
    Why it matters:
    Most people looking for a local agency search on a phone, and wrong details send them elsewhere.
    Fix:
    Update the Google listing today, then rebuild the site so it works on phones.
